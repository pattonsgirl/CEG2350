# PC Build Cliff Notes

Quick definitions of the terms that matter when picking parts, grouped by component.

Generated by Claude.

---

## CPU (Processor)

- **Cores / threads** – Independent processing units; threads let each core juggle two tasks. Games want 6–8 fast cores; editing and rendering scale with more.
- **Clock speed (GHz)** – Cycles per second. Boost clock is the short-burst peak. Only comparable within the same brand and generation.
- **IPC** – Instructions per clock, i.e. work done per cycle. Why a newer, lower-clocked chip can beat an older, faster-clocked one.
- **Cache (L3, 3D V-Cache)** – Small, very fast memory on the chip. Large L3 gives big gains in many games.
- **Socket** – The physical connector (e.g. AM5 for AMD, LGA1851 for Intel). CPU and motherboard must match.
- **TDP / PPT** – Rated power and heat output in watts. Guides cooler and PSU choice.
- **iGPU** – Integrated graphics. Lets you run a display without a graphics card; useful for troubleshooting.
- **Model suffixes** – Intel K = unlocked, F = no iGPU. AMD X = higher clocks, X3D = extra cache.

## Motherboard

- **Chipset** – Sets the board's feature tier (e.g. B-series vs Z/X-series). Higher tiers add overclocking, more lanes, USB, and M.2 slots.
- **Form factor** – ATX, Micro-ATX, Mini-ITX. Must fit your case; smaller means fewer slots.
- **VRM** – Power delivery to the CPU. Weak VRMs can throttle high-end CPUs.
- **PCIe lanes / generation** – Data highways to GPU and SSDs; each gen doubles bandwidth. Check which slots share lanes.
- **M.2 slots** – Slots for NVMe SSDs. Count them if you want several drives.
- **BIOS / UEFI** – Firmware and settings screen. Where you enable XMP/EXPO; may need an update for newer CPUs.
- **I/O** – Rear and front ports. Check for Wi-Fi, 2.5G Ethernet, enough USB, and front-panel headers.

## GPU (Graphics Card)

- **VRAM** – Memory on the card for textures and frames. Too little causes stutter at high settings and resolutions.
- **Resolution target** – 1080p, 1440p, or 4K. The biggest factor in how strong a GPU you need.
- **Ray tracing** – Realistic lighting simulation. Looks great, very demanding; performance varies by brand and generation.
- **Upscaling / frame generation** – DLSS, FSR, XeSS render lower and reconstruct, or insert generated frames. Big FPS gains; support varies by game.
- **Card dimensions** – Length and slot thickness. Measure case clearance first.
- **Power connectors** – 8-pin PCIe or 12V-2x6 (16-pin). PSU must supply the right connector and wattage.

## RAM (Memory)

- **Capacity** – 16GB is the gaming minimum; 32GB is comfortable; more for heavy creative work.
- **DDR4 vs DDR5** – Different generations, not interchangeable. The motherboard decides which.
- **Speed (MT/s)** – Transfer rate (e.g. 6000 MT/s). Faster helps up to what your CPU handles well.
- **CAS latency (CL)** – Delay before data returns. Lower is better; judge together with speed.
- **XMP / EXPO** – Profiles that run RAM at its rated speed. Off by default; enable in BIOS.
- **Dual channel** – Two sticks let the CPU read both at once. 2 × 16GB beats 1 × 32GB.

## Storage

- **NVMe SSD** – Fast solid-state drive over PCIe, usually M.2. Best for OS and games.
- **SATA SSD / HDD** – Slower interface; HDDs use spinning disks. Fine for bulk storage and backups.
- **PCIe Gen 3/4/5** – Sets max drive speed. Gen 4 is plenty for most people.
- **DRAM cache** – Onboard memory that helps the SSD sustain speed under heavy use.
- **TBW** – Terabytes written, the endurance rating. Rarely a concern for home use.

## PSU (Power Supply)

- **Wattage** – Maximum power output. Leave headroom above your estimated draw.
- **80 Plus rating** – Efficiency tiers: Bronze, Gold, Platinum, Titanium. Gold is a solid default.
- **Modular** – Fully modular cables detach; semi-modular has some fixed. Easier cable management.
- **ATX 3.x** – Newer standard that handles GPU power spikes and includes 12V-2x6. Recommended for current high-end GPUs.
- **Quality tier** – Community tier lists rank PSU quality. Don't cheap out; a bad PSU can damage other parts.

## Cooling

- **Air cooler** – Heatsink plus fan. Quiet, reliable, enough for most CPUs.
- **AIO liquid cooler** – Sealed pump, tubes, radiator (240/280/360mm). Better for hot CPUs; confirm the radiator fits the case.
- **Thermal paste** – Transfers heat from CPU to cooler. Often pre-applied.
- **Airflow** – Intake fans pull air in, exhaust fans push it out. Mesh-front cases run cooler.
- **Thermal throttling** – Parts slow themselves down when too hot. A sign cooling isn't keeping up.

## Case

- **Size** – Full tower, mid tower, small form factor. Determines what board, GPU, and radiator fit.
- **Clearances** – Max GPU length, CPU cooler height, radiator support. Check each against your parts.
- **Cable management** – Space and routing holes behind the motherboard tray. Cleaner build, better airflow.
- **Front I/O** – Front USB-A, USB-C, audio. Make sure the motherboard has matching headers.

## Big-Picture Concepts

- **Compatibility** – Socket, RAM type, form factor, clearances, and power must all line up. PCPartPicker flags most conflicts.
- **Bottleneck** – One part limiting the others (e.g. weak CPU holding back a strong GPU). Balance the budget for your use case.
- **FPS vs refresh rate** – Frames your PC renders per second vs how often the monitor updates (Hz). Match the GPU to the monitor.
- **Budget split** – For gaming, the GPU usually gets the largest share.
- **POST** – Power-On Self-Test, the first boot check. Motherboard debug LEDs point to the failing part.

## The Compatibility Chain

1. **CPU socket** → decides the motherboard
2. **Motherboard** → decides RAM type (DDR4/DDR5) and form factor
3. **Case** → must fit the board, GPU length, and cooler/radiator
4. **PSU** → must cover total power draw and provide the GPU's connector
